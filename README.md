# Tower of [Trials] - web demo (placeholder title)

A top-down 2D pixel-art action demo built with **Phaser 3** + **Vite**.
All art and sound is generated by code, so there are no image files to manage.
Works with keyboard + mouse, and with touch (phones/tablets).

## What is in the demo
- Tutorial Floor (traits, weapons, Mana Altar, training dummies, Trial of Bones)
- Floor 1 (preset dungeons, randomized dungeons, a hidden dungeon, the main dungeon)
- Double-dungeon rifts, levels 1-50, ranks F-S, traits + skills, Berserk / Sword Aura, Focus, Mana Circles
- Anything the plan left blank is shown as locked ("???" / "Coming soon")

Open the **DEMO** tab in the in-game menu for shortcuts (set level, force rifts, heal, etc.).
See `docs/ASSUMPTIONS.md` for every number I had to guess.

## Controls
| Action | Keyboard / mouse | Touch |
|---|---|---|
| Move | WASD / arrows | drag anywhere on the left (a stick appears under your thumb) |
| Aim | mouse | auto-aims at the nearest enemy; drag the ATTACK button to aim yourself |
| Attack / focus / cast | hold left click | hold ATTACK |
| Dodge | Space | DODGE (rolls the way you are running) |
| Skills 1-3 | 1 2 3 | tap the skill buttons |
| Berserk / Aura | R | AURA |
| Swap weapon | Q | SWAP |
| Use / open / read | E | USE (only shows up when something is in reach, with a label) |
| Full map | M | tap the minimap |
| Menu | Tab / Esc | MENU |
| Sound | N | menu > HELP / DEMO |

### Controller (Xbox / PlayStation / Switch Pro, standard layout)
Left stick or D-pad moves, right stick aims, RT or X attacks (hold = focus / cast), B or LT rolls, A uses / opens / reads, LB skill wheel, RB Aura, Y swaps weapon, R3 lock-on, Back full map, Start menu. In menus: D-pad moves the highlight, A presses, B closes, LB / RB change tab. Rumble follows hits (HELP tab turns it off).

## Not getting lost
- A **yellow objective bar** at the top always says what to do next ("Hit a dummy: hold ATTACK", "Enter the Trial of Bones" ...) with the distance.
- A **yellow arrow** at the screen edge points at the goal, and a pulsing ring shows it once it is on screen.
- A **minimap** with fog of war (tap it for the full map). Portals, exits and nearby enemies show on it.
- A **USE button** with a label ("OPEN LOADOUT", "ENTER DUNGEON") and a bobbing "!" over anything you can use.
- Short one-time tips appear when something new comes up.
- All of this can be switched off in **MENU > HELP** (goal arrow, minimap, auto-aim, zoom, touch buttons).

## Game feel (what was tuned)
Fast acceleration and even faster braking, a small first-frame kick and extra grip when turning, normalised diagonals,
corner assist (you slide around wall corners instead of snapping to them), input buffering for dodge/attack,
a quick eased dodge roll with afterimages, a small lunge on melee swings, tiny hit-stop on hits,
and a smoothed camera that looks slightly toward where you aim or move. Constants are at the top of `src/game/player.js`.

## Sound (all made in code, no audio files)
- Every weapon has its own sound: sword swings are light and airy, the claymore is low and heavy, the spear is a short sharp thrust, the bow twangs (deeper when charged), the crossbow clicks and thunks, the pistol cracks with a room tail, the book flutters, the staff hums up to a boom.
- Hits depend on what you hit (skeleton bone rattles, the dummy is a wooden knock) and crits add a bright sting. Each sound has a few variants and never repeats the same one twice in a row.
- Skeletons are audible before you see them (soft clatter that gets louder and pans as they close in) and their swing wind-up has its own tell sound. XP orbs play a rising note chain when you collect several quickly.
- Everything goes through a limiter and soft sounds duck under big events, so big fights stay clear. Press N to mute.

## Music (generated live in code, no audio files)
- Five tracks, all in D so they crossfade without clashing: **title** (68 BPM, D minor: slow tolling bell, soft voices, a music box calling the Tower's four-note motif D F A G), **hub** (86 BPM, D dorian: warm and steady, a small pulse-wave tune), **dungeon** (64 BPM, D minor: a drone, a pad, a lonely flute fragment, water drips, a far bell), **hidden vault** (60 BPM, dorian: the same bones with more bells) and **double-dungeon rift** (56 BPM, phrygian: a tritone shadow in the drone, more drips).
- Dungeon music is *layered*: exploring is almost nothing but atmosphere. When skeletons start hunting you a bass pulse and hats fade in, and in a real fight drums, an arpeggio and string stabs join, all on the same clock so nothing falls out of time. It drops back one step after about 3.5 s of calm.
- Moments: a deep toll when the gate opens, a bright chord when a dungeon is cleared, tolling bells when you fall.
- Mix: music sits well under the sound effects, steps back (muffled) while a menu is open, ducks for big moments, and uses the same limiter as the effects. MUSIC and EFFECTS sliders are in the pause menu under HELP; N mutes everything.
- Code: `src/logic/musictheory.js` (keys, chords, motif, progressions: pure data), `src/game/tracks.js` (what plays bar by bar), `src/game/musicengine.js` (instruments + scheduler), `src/game/music.js` (what the scenes call). `tests/music.test.js` checks every note is in key.

## Title screen
- A low-angle, tilted (Dutch-angle) view up the Tower: the camera stands on the path with the hero, so the stepped shaft, cornices, bricks and flagstones all race away toward the sky. It is ray-cast from code (`src/art/titleworld.js`, camera maths in `src/logic/titlelayout.js`), so the perspective is exact, then shown at a whole-number zoom so every pixel is the same size. On top: dithered dusk sky, moon, drifting clouds, mountains, mist, a teal beacon beam up the Tower's axis, embers, soul motes, bats that orbit the spire (behind and in front of it), torches, two skeleton guards and the hero seen from behind. It parallaxes with your pointer or finger.
- The Tower has 11 storeys (tutorial + 10 floors) and **one window lights up for every dungeon you have cleared**, so the title shows your progress. With a save: CONTINUE (with level / rank / kills) and NEW GAME (needs a second tap to erase the save). Without one: START.
- Pressing it plays a short sequence: guards' eyes flare, the door swings open, the camera pushes into the light, then the game starts (the tilted world also rights itself as the camera pushes in). Landscape puts the Tower on the left and the menu on the right; portrait puts the logo on top, the Tower in the middle and the menu at the bottom.

## Juice (feedback on hits, kills and rewards)
- Tiered on purpose: a normal hit = small spark + a few chips + a tiny freeze; a crit = more gold chips, a ring, a longer freeze and a camera punch; a kill = the longest freeze, a skull, ribs and a thigh bone tumble out the way the blow was travelling (bouncing and settling on the pixel grid), its weapon clatters down, a heap of bones, dust, ash and a teal soul wisp are left behind, and only a handful of pieces stay on the floor (capped, oldest fade first); the final kill of a dungeon adds a short slow-motion.
- Camera punches (whole pixels) along swings, shots and hits taken. Red screen-edge flash when hurt and a slow pulse + heartbeat when health is low.
- Kills drop gold XP orbs that bounce, settle, then fly to you; the XP bar pulses and a small +N XP tag appears by your level.

## Run it on your PC (Windows cmd)
Needs Node.js 20+ (https://nodejs.org). In the folder where the zip is:

```
tar -xf tower-of-trials-demo.zip
cd tower-of-trials-demo
npm install
npm run dev
```
`npm run dev` prints a local address (usually http://localhost:5173). Open it.

## Put it on GitHub Pages
Needs Git (https://git-scm.com) and a free GitHub account. The repo already contains a publishing recipe
(`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`), so GitHub builds and hosts the game for you on every push. Nothing in the code needs
your username or repo name.

1. Optional helper that sets up git and the remote for you (use your real username and the repo name you want):
   ```
   npm run connect -- YOUR-GITHUB-USERNAME YOUR-REPO-NAME
   ```
2. On https://github.com/new create an **empty** public repo with that name (no README, no .gitignore).
3. In that repo: **Settings > Pages > Source: GitHub Actions** (do this before the first upload).
4. Upload the code (a browser window opens the first time so you can sign in):
   ```
   git add -A
   git commit -m "Tower demo"
   git push -u origin main
   ```
5. Open the repo's **Actions** tab; after about a minute the run turns green and the demo is live at
   `https://YOUR-GITHUB-USERNAME.github.io/YOUR-REPO-NAME/`.

Update the live site: `git add -A`, `git commit -m "update"`, `git push`.
If the first run failed because step 3 came after step 4: Actions tab > the failed run > **Re-run jobs**.

Prefer no Actions? `npm run deploy` builds and pushes to a `gh-pages` branch instead; then choose
**Settings > Pages > Source: Deploy from a branch > gh-pages / (root)**.

## Other commands
- `npm run single` builds ONE self-contained file, `dist/tower-demo.html`, that you can open straight from a phone/PC or send to someone
- `npm test` runs the logic tests (stats, Berserk, Focus, mana chances, dungeon layouts)
- `npm run build` makes the `dist` folder
- `npm run preview` serves the built `dist` folder locally

## Project map
- `src/data/` - numbers and content from the plan (stats, weapons, traits, world)
- `src/logic/` - pure rules (stats, combat, progression, Berserk, Focus, dungeon layout)
- `src/art/` - pixel art drawn from code (sprites, tiles, font)
- `src/game/` + `src/scenes/` - the Phaser game (`music*.js`, `tracks.js`, `deathfx.js`; `scenes/titlebackdrop.js` is the animated title)
- `src/ui/` - menus and title screen (HTML overlay). The menu font `pixel-ui.ttf` is generated from the in-game glyphs (`node tools/build-font.mjs`, needs python3 + fonttools), so menus and game share one typeface

## Clutter rules (keep the middle of the screen clear)
- Popups are small plain text with no boxes: one toast at a time (queued), objective line is a single line, damage numbers are capped at 5 and merged.
- Everything in the world is drawn at the world's pixel size: the goal arrow is 16 pre-drawn pixel directions placed on the world grid, rings are whole-pixel frames, nothing is scaled by fractions.
- Names above altars/portals only appear when you are close.

## Enemy awareness (why skeletons react when they do)
- A skeleton only notices you when you are **in its own room** and it has a clear line of sight, or when you are right beside it (it hears you).
- Noticing takes a moment: a **?** shows while it is unsure (faster the closer you are), then **!** when it spots you. Walk back out of the room to stay hidden.
- Break line of sight for a few seconds and it gives up, searches the last place it saw you, then goes back to its post.
- Fighting is loud: attacking in a room makes the rest of that room suspicious; a spotted skeleton alerts its room-mates (walls stop the shout).
- Code: `src/logic/awareness.js` (pure, tested) + the states in `src/game/enemy.js`; numbers live in `src/data/enemies.js`.

## Controls layout (why the buttons are where they are)
- Round, see-through discs on a thumb arc around ATTACK: DODGE is closest (most used), skills on the outer arc in fixed slots, AURA/SWAP above, USE only appears near things.
- The rim colour says what a button does; labels disappear after a button has been used 8 times; the controls fade when you are idle and come back when enemies engage.
- In portrait the world fills the whole screen and the camera keeps the hero centred above the controls.
